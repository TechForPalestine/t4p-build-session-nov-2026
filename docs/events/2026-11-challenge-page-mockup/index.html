Incubator Challenge · November 2026

How do we expose and combat hasbara, pro-Israel bias and Zionist ownership and influence of mainstream news media?

This month we're forming a cohort of projects that expose how the news is shaped, and build ways to get it out that can't be bought.

What are the Tech for Palestine Challenges?

To date, the Tech for Palestine Incubator has supported more than 100 projects working on different aspects of Palestinian liberation. These projects span themes like media bias, protest tech, divestment, exposing complicit companies, and much more.

Applications for the Incubator are open on a rolling basis for any relevant idea which furthers the Palestinian cause. On top of that, each month we pose one open challenge to the community, to direct efforts towards priorities we consider urgent.

The projects which apply for the challenge and are approved to join the Incubator form a small cohort, with tailored capacity building and opportunities for close collaboration with the other projects in the cohort.

About the challenge

Hasbara has a newsroom. Palestine needs its own.

The mainstream media narrative on Palestine and the Gaza genocide is flooded with hasbara propaganda and consistently framed around Israel's talking points. It normalizes, downplays and manufactures consent for the atrocities Israel commits, through a playbook that is easy to spot once named.

The playbook

  1. 1 Passive voice

    Atrocities are reported without naming who committed them.

    Taken on by question Q03 , question Q04

  2. 2 Unequal coverage

    Israeli deaths and casualties get far more coverage than Palestinian ones.

    Taken on by question Q01 , question Q03

  3. 3 Selective doubt

    Fatality figures from Palestinian institutions are questioned, while Israeli claims are cited without question.

    Taken on by question Q05

  4. 4 Silenced newsrooms

    Journalists worldwide are penalized for positions sympathetic to Palestine, and newsroom guidelines restrict reporting, even simply using the word “Palestine”.

    Taken on by question Q06 , question Q09

  5. 5 Pro-Israel owners

    With Paramount's takeover of Warner Bros, some of the dominant US news operations are now owned by the Ellison family, known for its support for Israel and its million-dollar donations to Friends of the IDF.

    Taken on by question Q07 , question Q08

We urgently need independent media which can report on Palestine and Israel objectively, as well as transparency and public awareness of the positionality of different news sources.

This challenge asks you to find ways to combat the different aspects of large-scale, systemic pro-Israel influence over mainstream media: monitoring and exposing bias, raising awareness of hasbara and encouraging critical thinking, and limiting the influence of biased outlets over the public narrative.

We're especially interested in sovereign, independent ways to publish and disseminate news, including non-traditional channels that are harder to influence.

Some questions you can consider

Apply for this challenge →
  1. 01

    How do we ensure that the Palestinian struggle is top of mind for the general audience even if mainstream media tries to bury it?

  2. 02

    How do we develop alternative ways of sharing news using analog media, local distribution, peer-to-peer sharing and other channels which are resistant to outside control and influence?

  3. 03

    How do we detect and raise awareness about pro-Israel and/or anti-Palestine bias and framings in mainstream media?

  4. 04

    How do we encourage critical thinking among readers and viewers and ensure they are aware of the positionality of the news sources they are following?

  5. 05

    How do we fact-check claims in mainstream media and trace their origin to state PR messaging when they are the product of hasbara?

  6. 06

    How do we expose the unofficial editorial guidelines which media outlets use in their reporting on Palestine and detect regurgitations of Israeli talking points?

  7. 07

    How do we build a public ownership atlas of news media, showing who owns which outlet and their documented political donations and advocacy funding?

  8. 08

    How do we track the changes in coverage of Palestine and Israel by news outlets like CNN and CBS before and after the takeover by Paramount?

  9. 09

    How do we document and support journalists penalized for pro-Palestine reporting and encourage reporters to maintain journalistic integrity?

Already in the Incubator

Incubator projects we already support

  • N

    NewsCord

    NewsCord shows you the news on topics you care about, using something they call Cross-Source Fact Aggregation. That means it pulls together facts and angles from dozens of global sources, so you see a fuller and more accurate picture. For every story, you get three things: 1) The complete context. 2) A breakdown of where each part of the story comes from (local, Western mainstream, tabloid, Israeli, alternative, and more). 3) A side-by-side look at how different sources frame the same event, including when those narratives clash. NewsCord recently also launched a campaign tool that lets users send pre-written emails to media regulators, editors, and politicians in one click. Since its launch, the feature has gained significant traction, including a campaign that pressured eight major outlets like BBC, CNN, and The Guardian to account for omitting "anti-Muslim hate" from their coverage of a Prince Harry op-ed.

  • M

    Media-Bias-Meter

    Exposing anti-Palestine media bias through language, lies by omission and disproportionate coverage and deflection. We focus on NYT and Die Welt for our first releases

    Visit website ↗ (opens in a new tab)
  • M

    MediaBiasWatch

    Leveraging advertiser boycotts to influence biased media Media bias is inherently bad for society. Bias in the news we hear and see prevents citizens from making good choices, leading to waste – wasted lives, wasted health, spoiled environments, and lost opportunities. Many special interests groups compete to influence the information we receive to their benefit. The goal of this project is to highlight and shame advertisers who promote bad information, influencing media content-makers to make better choices. The project will take as an input credible processes which detect clear instances of bias, and turn them into effective advertiser boycotts.

    Visit website ↗ (opens in a new tab)
  • B

    Bias-Proof

    This project is a media bias highlighting tool for deterring the users from (blindly) reading articles from western/biased media sources. It analyzes the news articles on the fly and runs a deep search on them to find affiliations and motives that explain the basis for bias and can run fact checking to dent the credibility of these articles before a user reads it. This would save the readers' time from doing the research themselves and be guided to right information at the same time.
