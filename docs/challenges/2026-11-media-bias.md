# November 2026 challenge: hasbara and media bias

Source: https://techforpalestine.org/monthly-challenge, read 2026-11-16.

## The question

"How do we expose and combat hasbara, pro-Israel bias and Zionist ownership
and influence of mainstream news media?"

Page subtitle: "This month we're forming a cohort of projects that expose how
the news is shaped, and build ways to get it out that can't be bought."

T4P is "especially interested in sovereign, independent ways to publish and
disseminate news, including non-traditional channels that are harder to
influence."

## The nine prompts (Q01 to Q09)

Paraphrased; quote the page for anything public.

1. Keep Palestine in public view when mainstream media buries it.
2. Alternative news channels: analog, local distribution, peer-to-peer.
3. Detect and raise awareness of pro-Israel or anti-Palestine framing.
4. Help readers think critically and know where each source stands.
5. Fact-check claims and trace them back to state PR messaging.
6. Expose unofficial editorial guidelines and repeated talking points.
7. A public ownership atlas of news media, with owners' political donations.
8. Track CNN and CBS coverage before and after the Paramount takeover.
9. Document and support journalists penalised for pro-Palestine reporting.

The page maps these to five tactics it calls "the playbook": passive voice,
unequal coverage, selective doubt, silenced newsrooms, pro-Israel owners.

## Already in the Incubator

Hackathon teams should know these exist so they build alongside, not
duplicate:

- NewsCord (https://newscord.org/): side-by-side framing of one story, plus
  one-click email campaigns to outlets.
- Media-Bias-Meter (https://www.mediabiasmeter.com/): bias through language,
  omission and coverage share; NYT and Die Welt first.
- MediaBiasWatch (https://mediabiaswatch.org): turns proven bias into
  advertiser boycotts.
- Bias-Proof (techforpalestine.org/projects/bias-proof-236): flags bias in an
  article, looks up the outlet's affiliations, runs fact checks.
