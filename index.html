The challenge

How do we expose and combat hasbara, pro-Israel bias and Zionist ownership and influence of mainstream news media?

Hasbara has a newsroom. Palestine needs its own.

The mainstream media narrative on Palestine and the Gaza genocide is flooded with hasbara propaganda and consistently framed around Israel's talking points. It normalizes, downplays and manufactures consent for the atrocities Israel commits, through a playbook that is easy to spot once named.

We're especially interested in sovereign, independent ways to publish and disseminate news, including non-traditional channels that are harder to influence.

Some questions you can consider

  1. 01How do we ensure that the Palestinian struggle is top of mind for the general audience even if mainstream media tries to bury it?
  2. 02How do we develop alternative ways of sharing news using analog media, local distribution, peer-to-peer sharing and other channels which are resistant to outside control and influence?
  3. 03How do we detect and raise awareness about pro-Israel and/or anti-Palestine bias and framings in mainstream media?
  4. 04How do we encourage critical thinking among readers and viewers and ensure they are aware of the positionality of the news sources they are following?
  5. 05How do we fact-check claims in mainstream media and trace their origin to state PR messaging when they are the product of hasbara?
  6. 06How do we expose the unofficial editorial guidelines which media outlets use in their reporting on Palestine and detect regurgitations of Israeli talking points?
  7. 07How do we build a public ownership atlas of news media, showing who owns which outlet and their documented political donations and advocacy funding?
  8. 08How do we track the changes in coverage of Palestine and Israel by news outlets like CNN and CBS before and after the takeover by Paramount?
  9. 09How do we document and support journalists penalized for pro-Palestine reporting and encourage reporters to maintain journalistic integrity?

What teams can build

We want to see a working prototype or a campaign which addresses the challenge head on. Most of these need writing, research or design more than code:

  • A guide to spotting biased headlines
  • A letter campaign to one newsroom
  • A printable news sheet for community spaces
  • A first map of who owns major outlets
  • A before-and-after look at one outlet's coverage
  • A support page for penalized journalists
  • For developers: a browser plugin that flags framing

FAQ

Do I need to know how to code?

No. Campaigns, research, datasets, designs and plans all count.

Do I need a team or an idea?

No. Teams form at the start. If you have an idea, pitch it in 60 seconds and people can join you.

Do I need to be a Tech for Palestine member?

No. The build session is open to everyone. You need a Discord account.

Is there a prize?

No. Each team gets feedback on its pitch, and the goal is an Incubator application from every team.

Which tools can we use?

Any tools you like, including AI tools.

Will it be recorded?

No. Key points from the opening are posted as text on Discord.

Is there a code of conduct?

Yes. All attendees agree to the code of conduct on the Tech for Palestine Discord.

Can't make it on November 28?

Apply for the Incubator with your idea directly. Applications are open on a rolling basis.

Have an idea that gets Palestine's story past the gatekeepers?

Build it with us on Saturday, November 28, then bring it to the Incubator.
